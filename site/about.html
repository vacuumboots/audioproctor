The Problem

Alberta provincial assessments — including those delivered through Vretta — don't natively support audio accommodation in the way schools need them to. Teachers are expected to provide audio support for students with IEPs or designations, but the standard assessment delivery environment gives them no good tools to do it.

The workarounds are familiar: another adult in the room reading aloud, screen readers that weren't calibrated for assessment content, or, in many schools, an actual CD player with a disc that may or may not still work. None of these are consistent. None of them scale. And none of them are what the accommodation is supposed to look like.

At the same time, schools have moved almost entirely to managed Chromebooks. Google Forms handles lockdown. Vretta handles delivery. But MP3 files — the format provincial assessment audio comes in — won't play inside Google Forms. The accommodation gap isn't theoretical. It shows up every assessment window.

The Solution

AudioProctor was built to fill that gap. A teacher uploads an audio file, generates a short access code, and hands it to the student. The student enters the code in a Chrome extension on their Chromebook. A locked fullscreen player opens. The student listens, replays if needed, and when the assessment is done, the teacher provides an exit word to close the player.

No student accounts. No student data. No IT ticket per assessment. The extension is deployed once by IT through Google Admin Console and works across every Chromebook in the division from that point forward.

Built by someone in the room

AudioProctor was built by an IT administrator at a real Alberta school division who ran into this problem and couldn't find a solution that didn't create new privacy risks or require significant infrastructure. The tool reflects what actually happens in schools — how assessments get run, how teachers operate under time pressure, and what IT departments will and won't support.
